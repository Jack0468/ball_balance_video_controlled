# Decoder Trial (ConvTranspose2d vs. Upsample+Conv2d) — Analysis (2026-08-13)

Hard data and results only. For narrative on what was built/fixed and why, see `docs/PROJECT_LOGBOOK.md` (13/08/2026 entry).

Motivation: `ConvTranspose2d` requires a custom hls4ml layer for FPGA deployment; `Upsample`+`Conv2d` uses two natively-supported hls4ml layers. Comparing accuracy and parameter cost to inform that choice. All runs: `trial_decoder_upsample_vs_transpose.py`, 600-row stratified subset of Dataset 8, 8 epochs, `TrialSharedVisionBackbone` (encoder/ball_head identical to production; only `mask_head`/`heatmap_head` decoder swapped).

## Interpretation

- **Run 1 (3 seeds) was misleading on ball-tracking variance.** It showed `upsample_conv` with a ~4x higher ball-error std than `transpose` (9.6 vs 2.3px), driven entirely by one seed (43). Run 2 (5 seeds, same weights) shows the two decoders' ball-error std is statistically indistinguishable (7.3 vs 7.3px) — the apparent instability was small-sample noise, not a decoder-specific property. Same lesson as the augmentation trial's Run 2 (`ANALYSIS_2026-08-11.md`): a single seed's ranking can flip on noise alone; this project's default should be >=5 seeds before trusting a ranking, not 3.
- **Mask/heatmap loss weight has a large, consistent effect on ball-tracking variance, for both decoders.** Ball-error std drops from ~7.3px (weights 0.5/0.1) to ~1.2-1.7px (weights 0.25/0.05 and 0.1/0.02) for both `transpose` and `upsample_conv` — consistent with shared-encoder gradient competition between the ball task and the mask/heatmap tasks (the risk `ml_system_parameter_budget.md`'s Option A section anticipated). Reducing mask/heatmap weight resolves it symmetrically; it is not a lever that favors one decoder over the other.
- **`upsample_conv`'s mask IoU advantage over `transpose` holds at every weight setting tested**, 15 seed-runs per decoder across Runs 2-4: +0.379 IoU (weights 0.5/0.1), +0.251 IoU (0.25/0.05), +0.300 IoU (0.1/0.02). `transpose`'s mask IoU is also considerably less consistent across seeds at every weight setting (std 0.11-0.23 vs `upsample_conv`'s 0.06-0.15), including three individual seeds landing at 0.275-0.383 in Run 2.
- **At the lowest loss weights (Run 4), ball-tracking is statistically tied between decoders (31.5 vs 31.6px) while `upsample_conv` still holds a large mask IoU lead (0.680 vs 0.380).** This is the strongest evidence in the trial: once the ball-tracking instability is controlled for via loss reweighting, `upsample_conv` no longer costs anything on the ball-tracking axis and keeps most of its mask-quality advantage.
- Parameter cost is consistent across all runs: `upsample_conv` uses 91,140 params vs `transpose`'s 64,260 (+42%, +~53KB at 16-bit) — both comfortably inside the 612.5KB FPGA BRAM budget on their own.
